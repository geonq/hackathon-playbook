# Problem Framing

Do not lead with a solution pitch. Help the team learn the actual problem first.

Ask who experiences the problem, when it happens, what they do today, where the workflow hurts, and what a useful first result would be. Confirm data access, permissions, safety boundaries, and who can judge usefulness.

Write this statement with the team:

> **[Person] needs to [complete a task] when [trigger], but [current friction]. We will demonstrate [one narrow outcome] using [approved input], and evaluate it with [observable measure].**

Then capture one primary user, one end-to-end workflow, one approved example, a baseline or comparison, one success measure, one likely failure case, and what remains out of scope. If the task affects a consequential decision, keep a human responsible for review.

Use `templates/project-brief.md` when available. Do not claim that this framing or any chosen measure is an official judging criterion.
